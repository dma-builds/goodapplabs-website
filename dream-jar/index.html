Somewhere around six or seven, a child’s sense of what is possible for them starts to narrow.

Researchers have found that by six, girls are already less likely than boys to say their own gender is “really, really smart”, and that the jobs seven year olds pick look much like the ones seventeen year olds pick. (Bian, Leslie and Cimpian, 2017; Drawing the Future, 2018)

We want children to see themselves in their dreams, while everything still feels possible.

I’m a dad, and I wanted my kids to keep dreaming out loud. So I made a thing.

Your child says a dream out loud. A moment later, they see themself living it. Watch them light up and say, “that’s me!”

My promise to you:I will help you. Your child will see themself in their dreams and see that those dreams are possible.

Your job: help your child with what’s next. Some dreams will change. Some will be outgrown and set down. A few will be pursued with daring courage.

In families like ours, a child’s dream is precious, and we take it seriously.
